Miracle Whip — Operating Aide-Mémoire

Halifax & Dartmouth Amateur Radio Clubs · portable QRP whip · v0.1
Also covers near-identical commercial units such as the Moonraker Whizz Whip (HF/VHF/UHF).

What it is: A short telescoping whip on a small box with a variable loading / matching control (dial moves a tap on a toroidal inductor) and a male PL-259 that plugs into the radio’s SO-239. Designed for QRP portables (classic companion to FT-817-class rigs). Covers roughly HF through UHF when whip length and dial are set correctly — not a miracle of efficiency, but very convenient.

Hard limits

ItemGuidance
PowerKeep to QRP. Moonraker sheet: 10 W max (their note also mentions 25 W PEP SSB). Prefer ≤5–10 W and watch heat/SWR. Do not run a 100 W radio into this whip.
ConnectorMale PL-259 → radio SO-239 (or short coax jumper if you want the whip remote from the radio).
Whip~48 in / ~130 cm telescopic. Fully extend for most HF; shorten toward ¼-wave (or ¾-wave) on VHF/UHF.
CounterpoiseNot required for basic use. A ¼-wave wire clipped to the radio ground / chassis often helps on HF.

Quick start — HF

  1. Plug the whip into the radio (firm SO-239 mate; support the radio so the connector is not levered).
  2. Set power low (start at 1–5 W). Select band / frequency.
  3. Extend the whip fully for HF (unless you already know a shorter setting for that band).
  4. Receive first: turn the tuning dial for a peak in band noise or the signal you are listening to.
  5. Fine with whip: tweak telescopic length a little for a sharper receive peak if needed.
  6. Key briefly (CW carrier / FM / tune tone as your licence and mode allow) and use the radio’s SWR meter (or external meter) to touch up dial ± a click and whip length for lowest SWR.
  7. Write down dial + whip notes for that band (cheat sheet below).
Order that works: noise/signal peak on receive → short TX check → minimize SWR. Do not “tune by ear” on transmit at high power.

Quick start — VHF / UHF

  1. Set the dial to the VHF mark / “dot” (or top of the inductor range). On these bands the loading coil is largely bypassed / shunted; whip length does the work.
  2. Set whip near ¼-wave at the operating frequency (¾-wave is an option on 2 m if length allows — can show useful gain vs ¼-wave).
  3. Check SWR and trim whip length. Expect grounding / how you hold the radio to change the reading.

What the two controls do

ControlJob
Dial (tap)Selects turns on the loading / matching toroid. Main HF tuning. On VHF/UHF, park at the VHF position.
Whip lengthSets physical resonance. Fine-tunes HF after the dial; primary adjuster on VHF/UHF.

Realistic expectations

  • On upper HF (e.g. 20 / 15 / 10 m) you can often get near 1:1 with careful adjustment.
  • On 40 / 80 m, expect compromise (often nearer 2:1 in original reports). Efficiency is low — QSOs still happen when conditions / persistence cooperate.
  • This is a portable compromise antenna, not a substitute for a full-size dipole or vertical with a real radial system.

Optional improvements

  • Add a ¼-wave counterpoise for the band in use (clip to radio ground lug / chassis).
  • Short length of coax between radio and whip if the PL-259 sticks out awkwardly.
  • Extra tip wire (inverted-L style) can help lower HF sometimes — retune dial afterward; may fall outside range on some bands.

Troubleshooting

SymptomTry
No receive peak while turning dialFully extend whip; wrong band; poor connection; try outdoors / clear of metal.
SWR won’t come down on HFRe-peak on receive first; move dial one click either way; small whip change; add ¼-wave counterpoise; drop power.
SWR jumps when you touch the radioNormal for a whip with no/poor counterpoise — add a radial or change how the radio is grounded/held.
VHF SWR badDial on VHF mark; set whip nearer true ¼-wave; recheck with radio on a table vs hand-held.
Radio cuts back / heatsPower too high or SWR too high — stop, retune, reduce power.
